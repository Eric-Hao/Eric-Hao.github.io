# Editorial standards

Write from the original research, not from prior summaries. Begin with a concrete
problem the reader can understand, explain the mechanism using a minimal example
when it clarifies the idea, and then test that explanation against the paper's
evidence. Use headings that advance this specific argument rather than repeating
a generic outline across papers.

Every quantitative claim needs a traceable page, table, figure, or section in the
record's `evidence` array. Preserve model size, benchmark subset, scaffold, metric
direction, hardware, and speed/quality variants where they change the comparison.
Do not turn a within-paper observation into a universal performance claim. Report
inconsistencies explicitly and distinguish the authors' evidence from editorial
interpretation. A model card is not an independent evaluation.

Figures must be inspected against the original manuscript. Explain what to look
at, what the figure supports, and what it cannot establish. A conceptual index
cover is not evidence. A source gap is a reason to withhold an explanation, never
a reason to reconstruct one from the old article.

Style references: OpenAI research articles and index, Anthropic research essays, NVIDIA technical posts, the VISTA team project page, and Microsoft's ResearchStudio paper2blog editorial guidance. editorial judgment; no external skill was installed or executed for this rebuild.
